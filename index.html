A Step-by-Step Workshop Guide

You already have
something worth selling.

This guide takes you through three things in order: figure out your offer → prove it with real people → get found by strangers. Each phase builds on the last. Work through the steps in order and check them off as you go.

How to use this guide: Work through each step in order. Check off a step when you've completed it — the progress bar at the top tracks where you are. Some steps have templates you can copy and paste. Don't skip ahead; each step builds on the last.

Before You Begin

You probably think you don't have enough to teach. You're almost certainly wrong.

Most people who arrive at a guide like this carry a quiet belief that their experience doesn't count — that it's too ordinary, too niche, or that someone else already does it better. That belief is the real obstacle, and it has nothing to do with the quality of what you know.

Here's what's actually true: you've spent years — maybe decades — solving problems, learning systems, figuring things out the hard way. The people just behind you on that same path would pay to skip the struggle. That's all an offer is.

This guide doesn't ask you to invent something new. It asks you to look clearly at what you already know, find the person it helps most, and put it in front of them. Start there.

Phase 1

Shape Your Offer

5 steps · Estimated time: 60–75 minutes

01

List everything you already know how to do

You don't need to invent something new. You need to look at what you've already mastered. Decades of work experience, life skills, and hard-won lessons are things people will pay to learn from you.

Your action

Open a notes app — or grab pen and paper — and write at least 5 answers to each of these three questions. Don't overthink it. Just write what comes to mind.

  1. What have you done professionally for five or more years?
  2. What do friends and family ask you for help with?
  3. What have you learned the hard way — that you wish someone had just told you?
→Your best offer is usually hiding in something you consider "obvious" — because you've known it so long, you've forgotten others don't.

03

Write your customer profile

You can't find someone if you don't know who you're looking for. This takes 10 minutes and makes every step that follows easier — your posts, your messages, your conversations.

Your action — answer these 5 questions in writing
  1. Who is this person? Age range and life situation — for example, "women in their 50s who recently left their corporate job."
  2. What is the one problem keeping them up at night that your offer addresses?
  3. Where do they spend time online? Facebook groups? YouTube? LinkedIn? TikTok?
  4. What have they already tried that didn't fully work for them?
  5. What does their life look like after you help them? Be as specific and visual as you can.
→Not sure? Think of one specific real person you know who could use your help. Build the profile around them. You'll adjust it as you learn from real conversations.

04

Choose your offer type and set a founding price

There are three ways to package what you know. Pick one to start. Then decide on a price for your first few clients — not your forever price, just the one you charge while you're gathering proof that your work gets results.

Offer TypeWhat it looks likeBest if you…
1-on-1 Coaching RecommendedYou meet with someone on Zoom and guide them through their problemEnjoy talking with people and helping them solve problems in real time
Done-For-You ServiceYou do the task for them — editing, organising, writing, bookkeepingHave a skill that requires knowledge most people simply don't have
Digital Guide or WorkbookA written document or short course they follow on their ownWant to create something once and sell it many times
Set a founding price

What is a founding price? It's what you charge your first 3–5 clients while you're building proof. It's lower than what you'll charge long-term — but it is not free.

Why charge anything at all? When something is free, people don't show up, don't do the work, and don't give you useful feedback. A price creates commitment on both sides. Even $50 changes the dynamic.

A sensible starting range for 1-on-1 coaching: $97–$197 per session is a reasonable founding price. Check what others in your niche charge and price below that while you're getting started.

When to raise your price: After you have 3 testimonials that describe a clear, specific result, raise your price. Keep raising it as you build more evidence your work gets results. You don't need permission — you just do it.

→For most beginners: 1-on-1 coaching, founding price $97–$197. Zero technical setup, you learn fast what your customer wants, and you can raise your rate as soon as you have testimonials.

Prove It with Real People

06

Build your list of 10

Before you write a single message, you need a list of real names. Not a target market. Not a persona. Ten specific people who might benefit from your offer — or who know someone who would. The AI coach below will help you find them.

Where to look
  • Past work — former colleagues, clients, people you trained or managed
  • Community — church, sports clubs, volunteer groups, neighbours, hobby groups
  • Family and friends — and crucially, their extended circles ("do you know anyone who…")
  • Online — Facebook friends, LinkedIn connections, WhatsApp groups you're already in

07

Send your first messages

This step is not about selling. The goal is to start a genuine conversation and find out if your offer sounds interesting to real people. You are looking for curiosity and honest feedback — not a yes right now.

Many people feel awkward doing this. That's completely normal. The message below is deliberately low-pressure — you're not asking for a commitment, you're letting people know what you're doing and asking if they or anyone they know might benefit. A personal message to someone who knows you is not pushy. It's human.

Message template — send as a text, DM, or Facebook message
Hey [Name], hope you're doing well! I recently started offering [brief description of what you do] for [describe your ideal customer in 2–3 words]. I'm looking for a few people to work with as I get started — and I thought of you. If you or anyone you know might benefit from this, I'd love to have a quick chat. No pressure at all. Either way, hope life is treating you well!
→Send this as a personal message — not a mass email or group post. Personal messages get read. Send to all 10 names from Step 6, one by one.

→The goal of every reply is not to close the sale — it's to earn the next message. Ask one question. Be curious, not pushy.
08

Have the conversation

Someone is interested and you've arranged a quick chat. This doesn't need to be a formal presentation. It's a 20-minute conversation where you listen more than you talk — and then describe how you can help.

A simple structure for a 20-minute call
  1. Ask about their situation (5 min) — "Tell me a bit about where you are with [your topic] right now."
  2. Ask what they've tried (3 min) — "What have you already done about this?" Listen carefully — this tells you what they don't want to repeat.
  3. Ask what they want (3 min) — "If we could wave a wand, what would things look like in 3 months?" Let them paint the picture.
  4. Describe how you'd help (5 min) — Tell them specifically what you'd do together. Make it about their situation, not a generic pitch.
  5. State the price plainly (1 min) — "The way I work with people is [X]. My founding price is [Y]." Then stop talking. Let them respond.
  6. Answer questions honestly — If they hesitate on price, don't drop it immediately. Ask what feels off for them. Often it's not actually the number.
→The most common mistake: talking too much about yourself and too little about them. Your job is to understand their situation well enough that they feel genuinely heard — then show them how you can help.

Phase 3

Get Found by Strangers

8 steps · 30-day sprint — build a weekly rhythm

✦ Your saved offer
11

How reach works now

The old model was: build a following, then post to your following. That's slow. The shift over the last few years is that platforms like TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube now show content to people based on what they're interested in — not just who they follow. That changes the odds in your favour.

What changed — and what it means for you

What used to work: grow a following first, then post to them. Hope they engage and share.

What stopped working:

  • Organic reach on Facebook Pages collapsed around 2014–2016. A post to 1,000 followers might reach 30 people.
  • Instagram moved away from chronological feeds and prioritised accounts already getting high engagement — hard for new accounts to break through.
  • Posting without a clear strategy produced content that went nowhere.

What works now: interest-based distribution. Platforms like TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts decide who sees your content based on the topic — not your follower count. A good video from a brand-new account can reach thousands of the right people.

This doesn't make it effortless. The barrier is content quality and consistency, not account age. That's a much fairer game for a new creator.

Realistic expectations

Most people won't go viral. You don't need to. You need 5–10 paying clients, not 100,000 views. A post that reaches 300 of exactly the right people and leads to 3 enquiries is worth more than a post that gets 50,000 random views.

Plan for 3–6 months of consistent posting before you see it compound. The people who quit before that never find out what would have happened.

→The goal in Phase 3 is not to go viral. It's to show up consistently in front of the right people for 30 days. Everything else follows from that.
12

13

Join conversations before you post anything

Before you publish your first piece of content, spend two weeks just participating in communities where your customers already gather. You'll learn the exact language they use to describe their problems — and you'll become a familiar name before you ever ask for anything.

How to do this well
  1. Find 3–5 communities. Facebook Groups, Reddit threads, LinkedIn communities, or YouTube comment sections on videos your customer watches. Search for the problem your customer has, not your solution.
  2. Read the rules before you post. Most groups ban self-promotion. Getting removed loses you credibility with exactly the people you're trying to reach.
  3. Answer questions fully. When someone asks a question you can genuinely help with, write a complete answer. Not a teaser. Not "DM me for more." The full answer. People who give the most help earn the most trust.
  4. Do this for 2 weeks before posting anything promotional. The goal is to become known as someone who gives before they ask.
What you'll take from these two weeks

The exact words and phrases your customers use to describe their own problem. Use their language — not yours — in your content, your offer, your bio. This is primary research you can't get any other way.

→Your action this week: join 3 communities. Spend 15 minutes a day reading and answering questions. Don't mention your offer at all for the first two weeks.
14

Build a content rhythm you can actually keep

The biggest mistake new creators make is starting too fast and burning out. Start with 3 posts a week. That's enough to build momentum without it becoming unsustainable after a fortnight.

What to post — the 4:1 rule

For every post that mentions your offer, publish four that don't. This is the ratio that builds trust.

  • Jab posts (the four): Answer a question you got this week. Explain a mistake you see often. Share a tip from your own experience. Break down a common misconception. "What I wish I'd known when I started [your topic]."
  • Hook post (the one): "If you want help with this, here's how I work with people." Link in bio or DM me. Calm and clear — no pressure.
Use AI to start, your voice to finish

It's fine to use Claude or ChatGPT to draft your first posts. Describe your offer, your customer, and the topic you want to cover. Then read the draft out loud. Change every sentence that doesn't sound like you. Your authentic voice is more trustworthy than polished but generic text.

15

Record your first video — you don't have to be on camera

Short-form video reaches people faster than any other format. But you don't have to show your face. Many people start without being on camera at all — especially while building confidence.

Non-camera options that work well
  • Slides with your voice over the top. A simple presentation narrated on your phone. Make it in Canva. Record yourself talking through each slide.
  • Point the camera at something else. Show what you're doing — cooking, walking, working at a desk — and talk over it.
  • Screen recording with voiceover. Show your phone or laptop screen while you explain a concept.
  • Text captions over a still image. Your words appear as captions on screen. No face required. Works well on TikTok and Instagram.

When you're ready to show your face, the setup is simple: phone propped up, natural window light in front of you, no editing needed. Authenticity outperforms production quality on short-form platforms.

Structure for a 30–60 second video
  1. Hook (first 3 seconds): A specific question or bold statement your customer will immediately recognise. "If you're struggling with [problem], here's why."
  2. Value (20–40 seconds): Give the actual tip. One clear point. No long introduction about yourself.
  3. Close (5 seconds): A soft invitation. "If you want help with this, my link is in my bio." Or ask a question to invite a comment.

16

Turn viewers into conversations

Views don't pay you — conversations do. This step closes the gap between someone watching your video and someone actually reaching out to you.

Four things that move viewers to conversations
  1. Your bio should do one job. Who you help and what they get — not your job title, not "passionate about fitness." Something like: "I help women 50+ lose weight without giving up the foods they love — DM me 'ready' to get started."
  2. Ask people to comment a keyword. In your video or caption, say: "Comment 'guide' below and I'll send you my free [thing] directly." When they comment, send them a DM. A public comment becomes a private conversation — and sales happen in private conversations.
  3. Have a free resource ready. A one-page PDF, a checklist, a short guide. Something specific and useful. It gives people a low-stakes reason to start talking to you before they're ready to buy.
  4. Capture contact details. Once you're in DMs, invite people onto your email list or WhatsApp broadcast. Your social accounts can get restricted — your email list and WhatsApp list belong to you.
→If someone replies to a DM and you're not sure what to say, use the Sales Reply Coach in Step 7. It works for social DMs too, not just cold outreach.
17

Borrow someone else's audience

The fastest way to reach new people is to appear in front of an audience someone else has already built. You don't need a large following to do this — you need to offer genuine value to their audience.

Four ways to do this
  1. Guest in a Facebook Group. Offer to run a free 30-minute Q&A or mini-workshop for a group where your customers gather. The admin gets valuable content for their members. You get direct access to your ideal audience.
  2. Co-hosted live session. Find someone with a complementary offer — not a competitor — and run a joint live. You promote it to your list, they promote it to theirs.
  3. Podcast interview. Search for podcasts in your niche with 1,000–10,000 listeners. Smaller shows are easier to get on and often have more engaged listeners. One good interview can bring in 5–10 qualified enquiries.
  4. Collaboration post. Tag another creator in a post that features both your perspectives on the same topic. They share it. Simple and low-effort.
Pitch message — for reaching out to group admins or podcast hosts
Hi [Name], I've been a member of [group / listener of your podcast] for a while and genuinely value what you've built here. I help [who you help] with [what you help with], and I think it would be useful to your audience. I'd love to offer a free [30-minute Q&A / interview / live workshop] on [specific topic they'd find useful]. I won't pitch my services during it — this would be purely useful content for your members. Happy to do a quick chat first if you'd like to know more. Let me know if this feels like a good fit.
→Send one outreach this week. One. Then go back to posting your regular content. Don't wait for a reply before you take the next step.
18

Run a free live session — then review your week

A free live session is one of the most useful things you can do in your first 90 days. People experience your expertise before they buy. You have a direct conversation with your audience. And you learn exactly what they need in their own words.

How to structure 45 minutes
  1. Introduce yourself briefly (3 min) — who you are and what you'll cover today. Not your whole story.
  2. Teach one complete idea (25 min) — a specific, actionable topic your customer struggles with. Give the whole answer, not a teaser. Giving away real value is what builds trust.
  3. Take live questions (12 min) — write down every question. They're future content and future offer ideas.
  4. One clear invitation (5 min) — "If you want to work through this with me, here's how." One option. One link. Calm and clear.
Invitation post — to promote your free session
I'm running a free live [class / workshop / Q&A] on [date] at [time]. Topic: [specific thing you'll cover — e.g. "Why your past diets failed and what to do instead"] This is for you if: [1-line description of who it's for] I'll cover [what they'll leave knowing]. No pitch, no upsell — just useful. [Platform link] — drop a comment or DM me to get the link.
→Run your first one even if only 5 people show up. The recording becomes content you can reuse. Every live session gets easier.
