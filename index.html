A Step-by-Step Workshop Guide

You already have
something worth selling.

This guide walks you through three things in order: figuring out what to offer, setting up a way to get paid, and finding your first customers — including how to use today's social platforms to reach people who've never heard of you.

How to use this guide: Work through each step in order. Check off a step when you've completed it — the progress bar at the top tracks where you are. Some steps have templates you can copy and paste. Don't skip ahead; each step builds on the last.

Before You Begin

You probably think you don't have enough to teach. You're almost certainly wrong.

Most people who arrive at a guide like this carry a quiet belief that their experience doesn't count — that it's too ordinary, too niche, or that someone else already does it better. That belief is the real obstacle, and it has nothing to do with the quality of what you know.

Here's what's actually true: you've spent years — maybe decades — solving problems, learning systems, figuring things out the hard way. The people just behind you on that same path would pay to skip the struggle. That's all an offer is.

This guide doesn't ask you to invent something new. It asks you to look clearly at what you already know, find the person it helps most, and put it in front of them. Start there.

Phase 1

Discover What to Sell

4 steps · Estimated time: 45–60 minutes

01

List everything you already know how to do

You don't need to invent something new. You need to look at what you've already mastered. Decades of work experience, life skills, and hard-won lessons are things people will pay to learn from you.

Your action

Open a notes app — or grab pen and paper — and write at least 5 answers to each of these three questions. Don't overthink it. Just write what comes to mind.

  1. What have you done professionally for five or more years?
  2. What do friends and family ask you for help with?
  3. What have you learned the hard way — that you wish someone had just told you?
→Your best offer is usually hiding in something you consider "obvious" — because you've known it so long, you've forgotten others don't.

02

Find the problem that matches your skill

People don't buy skills — they buy relief from a specific frustration. Your job here is to connect what you know with a problem someone else is losing sleep over.

Your action

Take one item from your Step 1 list and fill in this sentence:

Fill in the blank
"I help [type of person] who [describe their struggle] to [the result they want] — without [the thing they're afraid of]."
Example

"I help recently retired women who feel anxious about their finances to understand and manage their money — without needing to hire a financial advisor."

→The more specific, the better. "I help recently retired women who feel anxious opening their bank statements" is something people recognize themselves in — and respond to.
03

Set Up to Accept Payment

4 steps · Estimated time: 30–45 minutes

06

Create your Gumroad account

Gumroad is a simple platform where you list your offer, share a link, and get paid — no website required, no technical skills needed. The whole signup takes about five minutes.

Step by step
  1. Open your browser and go to gumroad.com
  2. Click the "Start Selling" button
  3. Enter your name, email address, and a password you'll remember
  4. Open your email inbox and click the confirmation link Gumroad sends you
  5. You'll arrive at your Gumroad dashboard — you're now set up to sell
  6. Click Edit Profile and add a short one-sentence bio about what you do
→Use your real first name and a professional-sounding email address. Something like yourname@gmail.com works perfectly well.
07

Create your first product listing

This is the link you'll share when someone asks how to work with you. Clear and honest is what matters. You can always update it later.

Step by step
  1. From your Gumroad dashboard, click "New Product"
  2. Choose "Service" as the product type (for a coaching session)
  3. Give it a clear name — specific beats clever:
    "60-Minute Personal Finance Review Session" works better than "Coaching Call"
  4. Set your price. For 1-on-1 coaching, $97–$197 is a reasonable starting point
  5. Write 3–5 sentences in the description: who this is for, what they'll walk away with, and what happens after they buy (e.g., "You'll receive a booking link within 24 hours")
  6. Click "Publish" and copy your product link
→Don't let perfectionism stall you. A live listing you update later beats a perfect one that never launches.
08

Connect your bank account to receive payments

When someone buys from you, Gumroad collects the money and sends it to your bank account. You need to tell it where to send your earnings.

Step by step
  1. In your Gumroad dashboard, click your profile icon in the top right corner
  2. Select Settings from the dropdown
  3. Click Payouts in the left sidebar
  4. Click "Add a Bank Account"
  5. Enter your bank's routing number and your account number — find both at the bottom of a paper check, or in your bank's app under "Account Details"
  6. Save your information

What to expect: Your first actual payout typically arrives 7–21 days after your first sale. This delay is normal and won't repeat once your account is established.

→Don't have your routing number? Call your bank's customer service line or search "[your bank name] routing number" — it's public information.
Phase 3

Find Your First Customers

7 steps · Estimated time: Ongoing — start this week

09

Write your customer profile

You can't find someone if you don't know who you're looking for. This exercise takes 10 minutes and makes every step that follows easier — your posts, your messages, your conversations.

Your action — answer these 5 questions in writing
  1. Who is this person? — Age range, life situation (e.g., "women in their 50s who recently left their corporate job")
  2. What is the ONE problem keeping them up at night related to what you offer?
  3. Where do they spend time online? — Facebook groups? TikTok? LinkedIn? YouTube?
  4. What have they already tried that didn't fully work for them?
  5. What does their life look like after you help them? — Be as specific and visual as you can
→If you're not sure, think of one specific real person you know who could use your help. Build the whole profile around them. You can adjust as you learn from real conversations.

10

Your first customers are already in your phone

Before you post anything publicly, reach out personally to people who already know and trust you. This is the fastest path to a first sale — and the step most people skip out of unnecessary embarrassment. A warm, personal message is not pushy.

Your action

Make a list of 10 people who might need your offer or know someone who does. Think: former coworkers, neighbors, church members, Facebook friends, LinkedIn connections.

Then send each of them a message like this — as a text, Facebook message, or DM:

Message template
Hey [Name], hope you're doing well! I recently started offering [brief description of what you do] for [describe your ideal customer in 2–3 words]. I'm looking for a few people to work with as I get started — and I thought of you. If you or anyone you know might benefit from this, I'd love to have a quick chat. No pressure at all. Either way, hope life is treating you well!
→Send this as a personal text or DM — not a mass email. Personal channels get read; bulk messages get ignored.

11

Understand the new rules — followers don't matter anymore

Before you post anything online, you need to understand how social platforms actually work in 2026 — because the rules changed, and the change is completely in your favor.

"We do not live in 'social media' anymore. You're getting content based on what you're currently interested in — not who you follow."

— Gary Vaynerchuk, 2026
What this means for you

Platforms like TikTok, Instagram Reels, and Snapchat Spotlight are now interest-based. Their algorithms show your content to people who are interested in your topic — not just your followers. This means:

  • You do not need thousands of followers to reach thousands of people
  • A genuinely useful first post can reach your ideal customer on day one
  • Your age, follower count, and technical skills are not the barrier you think they are

Gary proved this by launching a brand new TikTok account with zero followers and getting 8.2 million organic views on the first post. The content was good and relevant. That's the only thing that mattered.

→Read this step twice if you need to. The mindset shift — from "I need an audience first" to "good content finds its own audience" — is the most important thing in Phase 3.

13

Your content playbook — jabs first, hooks occasionally

Gary Vaynerchuk built his entire philosophy around one principle: give away value before you ask for anything in return. He calls this "jabs and hooks" — and it works because it matches how trust actually forms between people.

The framework

Jabs = free value. Tips, insights, answers to common questions, short lessons, behind-the-scenes moments. No mention of your offer. Just be genuinely helpful.

Hooks = your offer. "Here's how to work with me." "Link in bio." "Book a session." Direct and clear.

The ratio: For every 1 hook, post 4–5 jabs. Most new entrepreneurs do it backwards — they only post when they want to sell something, and people tune it out.

Three rules that matter more than anything else
  1. Post organically first, boost later. Never spend money promoting a post until you've seen it perform for free. If it gets no traction without a budget, an ad budget won't fix it. Only boost content that's already resonating.
  2. Consistency beats perfection. One post every day beats one perfect post every month. Gary produces 500+ pieces of content monthly for his own brand. You don't need to hit that number — but showing up regularly is non-negotiable.
  3. Use AI to start, your voice to finish. Use Claude or ChatGPT to generate your first drafts. Then read them out loud. Change every sentence that doesn't sound like you. Your imperfect, authentic voice is more powerful than polished but generic copy.

14

Record your first short-form video — simpler than you think

Short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, Snapchat Spotlight) is the single most powerful way to reach new people for free in 2026. Many people feel intimidated by this — but the videos that work best are not polished or produced. They're honest, direct, and useful.

The format that works — no editing required

Pick up your phone. Find a window with natural light. Point the camera at your face. Record 30–60 seconds using this structure:

  1. Hook (first 2–3 seconds): Start with a bold statement or question that your customer will immediately recognize. The first 3 seconds decide whether anyone watches the rest.
  2. Value (20–40 seconds): Give the actual insight. One clear tip. No filler. No long introduction about who you are.
  3. Close (5 seconds): A soft invitation — "If you want help with this, my link is in my bio."
Script template — fill in for your topic
[Hook] "The #1 mistake [people like your customer] make when trying to [your topic] is..." [Value — give the real answer] "Most people think you need to [common wrong assumption]. But actually, what works is [your insight]. Here's why..." [Close] "If you're dealing with this and want some help, I work with people one-on-one — link is in my bio."
Technical setup — keep it simple
  • Phone: Any smartphone from the last five years works perfectly
  • Light: Sit facing a window. Natural light is all you need.
  • Sound: Find a quiet room. Your phone's microphone is fine to start.
  • Editing: Do not edit. Post the raw video. Your first 10 videos are practice. Authenticity outperforms production quality on these platforms every time.
→The "imperfect but real" video almost always outperforms the heavily edited one. People follow people, not productions. Press record, say the useful thing, and post it as-is.
15

Make your first sale — then ask for a testimonial

Everything before this was preparation. This is the step that makes you an entrepreneur. When someone says yes, here's exactly what to do — and what to ask for afterward.

When someone expresses interest
  1. Reply quickly and warmly — enthusiasm from you breeds confidence in them
  2. Send them your Gumroad product link:
    "Just click this link, complete checkout, and I'll reach out within 24 hours to schedule our session."
  3. Deliver your very best work — this person is also your first case study
  4. A day or two after the session, send this message:
Testimonial request message
Hi [Name], I really enjoyed our session — thank you for trusting me with this. If you found it helpful, would you be willing to send me a short note about your experience? Even 2–3 sentences would mean a great deal. I share these with people who are thinking about working with me. No rush — only if it feels right to you!
→Screenshot their reply and save it. Your first testimonial is worth more than any advertisement. Add it to your Gumroad listing and share it (with their permission) in your videos and posts.
